# 2D forced-flow extension: fixed main protocol

Specified after numerical-design pilots on seed 9001, before generating the main
test data or observing main model superiority. This is a new extension of the
course project, not a reproduction of a 2D experiment in the original paper.

## Questions

1. Does an emulator trained exclusively on a rough 2D solver beat that solver
   against an independently refined reference, over a predefined forecast window?
2. Does four-step training change the result when the underlying teacher
   trajectories remain identical to those used for one-step training?
3. If the emulator has a lower field-error score, does it retain physical flow
   structure, energy, and small-scale content, or merely damp out a chaotic field?

No desired sign of the outcome is assumed. Negative, unstable, and seed-dependent
outcomes remain in the final report.

## Physical and numerical setup

- Periodic square of side 2*pi, incompressible 2D Navier-Stokes in vorticity form.
- Kolmogorov forcing at y-wavenumber 4, forcing scale 1, viscosity 0.01, linear
  drag -0.1. This is a modest-Re forced-flow benchmark (forcing-based Re=100),
  not a simulation resolving industrial/high-Re turbulence.
- Rough teacher: 32x32 grid, ETDRK2, 10 substeps per recorded step of dt=0.2.
- Reference: 96x96 grid, ETDRK4, 20 substeps per recorded step. Independent
  refinement: 128x128 and 40 substeps, evaluated on all test cases for steps 1-20,
  and a predetermined first-eight subset for the longer horizon where practical.
- All solvers use the same viscosity, drag and forcing. Galerkin projection
  after each internal step retains |kx|,|ky| <= floor(N/3)-1 and removes the mean.
  This makes the common resolved band explicit and avoids retaining aliased modes.
- Initial fields: random multimode, zero-mean noise, randomized initial RMS in
  [0.5,1.5], then 60 physical time units of rough-solver warmup. All data,
  including warmup and training targets, are generated by the rough solver.
  No per-field height or RMS normalization is used for training or evaluation.
- Every fine reference starts from a Fourier interpolation of the identical
  coarse initial field. There is no hidden unresolved initial state.
- Main comparisons use the fine solution projected to the common coarse band.
  Full-fine-field errors are also reported, including unresolved reference power.
  A coarse model is not credited with predicting details it cannot represent.

## Data and model

- Two independent training datasets: seeds 1101 and 1102, each with 128 separate
  warmed-up initial flows and eight recorded teacher steps per trajectory.
- Independent common teacher-validation data: seed 2101, 32 flows, eight steps.
- Main test: seed 3101, 64 independent flows, 100-step forecasts. Test targets
  are never used for training, checkpoint selection, or hyperparameter choices.
- Residual 2D Fourier neural operator: width 16, ten spectral modes, four
  blocks, GELU, periodic mean-zero Galerkin projection. Input channels are
  current vorticity and the known fixed forcing field. Output is an increment
  added to the current field. Scale is one constant RMS estimated from the
  corresponding training set, shared across its recipes and initialization seeds.
- Supervised N=1 versus N=4, shared underlying H=8 teacher trajectories.
  Sliding windows differ in number and target-time weighting; update count and
  batch size are matched, but compute and optimization noise are not identical.
- Initialization seeds 0,1,2 crossed with the two training datasets and two
  recipes: 12 main trained models in total.
- Each gets 12,000 Adam updates, batch eight, peak learning rate 0.001,
  500 warmup updates, cosine decay to 0.00001, global gradient clipping at 1.
  Final prescribed checkpoint is reported, including unfavorable outcomes.
- A teacher-only fit pilot can check learning quality on disjoint pilot seeds;
  it is not pooled with main results. Any subsequent protocol change must be
  documented as an amendment, applied to all affected cells, without test tuning.

## Outcomes and controls

- Primary: sum of mean resolved-vorticity nRMSE over steps 1-20 divided by the
  teacher's corresponding sum (physical time 0.2-4). Ratio <1 favors the emulator.
- Secondary: steps 21-100 separately; full 1-100 ratio; absolute RMSE; velocity
  error; full-fine-field error; correlation with reference; kinetic energy,
  enstrophy, and radial energy spectra. Do not select a favorable individual step.
- Per-example paired trajectory bootstrap intervals, conditional on each model;
  dataset and initialization ranges are reported separately. Shared test cases
  across models are not treated as independent additional samples.
- Non-learning controls: persistence, zero field, and forced linear diffusion
  without nonlinear advection. These detect apparent long-horizon improvement
  obtained merely by forgetting or smoothing the flow.
- Teacher imitation uses identical held-out states and one-step targets across
  recipes. Loss curves and full update budgets are retained.
- Error mechanism: decompose squared-error change using e_model=e_teacher+delta.
  Report whether the learned deviation cancels teacher error or adds to it,
  together with energy and spectral diagnostics. This analysis is post-training
  and is not used to select a model.
- Record any nonfinite forecast and first error explosion. Do not silently
  filter unstable runs out of medians or counts.

## Validation and reporting limits

The design pilot checks diverse mature fields and finite solver rollouts.
Pilot and main-data reference sensitivities are recorded independently.
Check Fourier transfer, analytic Taylor-Green decay, mean and
spectral constraints, time refinement, and resumed Adam equivalence.

The primary question concerns short-term prediction relative to a converged
numerical reference. Long-term chaotic fields can decorrelate even for good
solvers. A lower pointwise error after decorrelation does not by itself imply
better physical fidelity. We do not claim a developed inertial range, universal
superiority, broad parameter generalization, or exact continuum truth.

### Preflight implementation correction

Before main training, analytic Taylor-Green decay exposed that Exponax 0.1.0
applies its documented drag twice in two spatial dimensions, by summing
derivative_operator**0 over axes. The extension overrides the linear symbol to
use the declared scalar drag exactly once. Pre-correction pilot artifacts were
quarantined under `results/turbulence_preflight_drag_2026_11_23/`; all main data
were regenerated with the corrected equation. This preserves the physical
protocol above. No main model or outcome existed when the correction was made.

### Teacher-only model-design amendment, before main training

The small-network pilot (width 12, modes 8, blocks 3; 3,000 updates, seed 9002)
had held-out normalized one-step imitation MSE 0.0162. A larger fitting pilot
(width 16, modes 10, blocks 4; 6,000 updates, seed 9003) reduced it to 0.00223.
Both use separate pilot training/validation flows (seeds 9101/9102) and no fine
targets. Architecture, budget, and initialization all differ, so this is a
practical model-design check, not an isolated architecture ablation. The larger
architecture above is fixed for all 12 main runs, each with 12,000 updates and
128 training flows. Main test reference data existed at amendment time, but no
main model superiority was available or used to make this choice.

### Secondary numerical controls

After the first two main one-step models finished, we added two evaluation-only
controls: an ETDRK4 time-refined solve on the same 32-grid, and an ETDRK4 48-grid
solve, both at 20 substeps per recorded interval. Their outputs are projected to
the same coarse band. These diagnose time versus spatial truncation effects
and provide a positive numerical-improvement control. They do not change the
main training protocol, references, models, or primary metric.

## Completion record

All 12 prescribed models completed 12,000 updates on November 24, 2026, with
finite 100-step forecasts. No model, seed, or checkpoint was selected using test
superiority. Workers interrupted by execution-environment disconnections resumed
the saved model and Adam states. The audit verifies all expected outcomes,
unchanged executed numerical sources, data and model hashes, recalculated primary
scores, Fourier full-field error reconstruction, and spectral energy consistency.
Final discussion and report-ready paragraphs are recorded with the results.
